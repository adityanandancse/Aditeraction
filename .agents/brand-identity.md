# Visual Identity Brief — Aditeraction

*Grounded in `.agents/brand-context.md`. No `brand-strategy.md` existed at time of writing. This brief is written for a designer who has not seen the brand — every direction is meant to be executed from, not interpreted loosely.*

---

## 01 — IDENTITY STRATEGY STATEMENT

Aditeraction is a design-and-engineering studio where the same hands that draw the brand also ship the code — so the identity has to look like proof, not decoration. The visual system should feel like a dark, cinematic stage with a single confident spotlight: precise enough to read as engineering, bold enough to read as a point of view, playful enough that it never tips into corporate stiffness or template-agency sameness. Every decision answers to one idea — *design and code are one discipline* — expressed literally in a type system that pairs a crafted grotesque with a monospace, and in a mark built on the moment two things lock into one. It must carry equal weight for a founder in Bengaluru and one in London, so it leans on geometry, restraint and motion rather than any regional or trend-driven cliché.

---

## 02 — LOGO DIRECTION

**Primary direction (LOCKED)** — **Symbol + wordmark.** A compact, ownable symbol — the **"code nib" mark** — paired with the wordmark **"aditeraction"**. The symbol must stand alone as an app icon / favicon; the lockup is the default for most applications. Master files live in `D:\Aditya\Aditeraction\brand\`.

**The concept (LOCKED)** — The mark is the **code nib**: code brackets `<` `>` framing a solid Vermilion pen nib. It fuses the two disciplines into one glyph — the brackets are engineering, the nib is design — so the symbol literally says "we design it and we code it." It reads as a distinctive constructed glyph, not a letter, cut cleanly on a grid.

**Character** — Precise, deliberate, quietly bold. A mark that looks engineered — like it snapped into place — with one small move that keeps it from feeling cold. Confident at rest, alive in motion.

**Style notes** — Constructed, not hand-drawn. Geometric with a tight optical grid. Minimal stroke count (survives 16px). Single weight, solid fills or consistent stroke — **no** gradients, **no** glow, **no** 3D bevels baked into the logo itself (3D/cinematic treatment lives in the *hero environment*, never in the mark). Sharp or minimally-rounded corners for a precision feel. The wordmark: **"aditeraction" set in Space Grotesk SemiBold, outlined, tracking −0.035em, with both i-dots rendered as Vermilion squares** — so it isn't just a font typed out.

**Animation behaviour** — The brackets should be able to *draw in and lock* around the nib (web hero, video intro), and the nib / i-dot squares can carry a restrained caret-style pulse to signal "interaction." Motion must feel engineered — eased, exact, purposeful — never bouncy or decorative.

**What to avoid** — Generic "A" monograms; rockets, lightbulbs, gears, brains; swooshes and speed-lines; AI-style gradient blobs, glows and iridescence; paisley, temple or other region-specific motifs; infinity loops; cursor/pointer clichés rendered literally; anything that resembles an existing studio or tech mark. No mark that only works in colour or only at large size.

**3 Logo References** (borrow the quality, not the look):
- **Linear** — borrow the *reductive geometry that scales to a favicon and animates cleanly*. The symbol is tiny, single-weight, and instantly legible at 16px; aim for that discipline.
- **Stripe (wordmark)** — borrow the *confident, precise-but-warm custom grotesque*. The letterforms feel engineered yet approachable — the exact register for Aditeraction's wordmark.
- **Nothing** — borrow the *system logic*, not the dot-matrix itself: the sense that one underlying grid/rule makes everything instantly recognisable across touchpoints. Aditeraction should have that kind of ownable structural signature.

## 03 — COLOR PALETTE

**Palette type** — Neutral-dominant with a single bold chromatic accent (near-monochrome structure + one signal colour). This is the restraint of Linear/Nothing with one decisive hit of personality.

**Primary / Signal color** — **Aditeraction Vermilion — `#FF4D2E`.**
A warm electric red-orange. It reads bold and straight-talking, carries energy without the coldness of the default tech-blue everyone else owns, and is culturally neutral enough to work identically for Indian and international clients. On the dark canvas it behaves as a solid spotlight — no glow needed. Used sparingly: one accent per view (a link, the active state, the mark's node, a key word).

**Structural neutrals**
- **Ink / Canvas — `#0B0B0D`** — near-black, the cinematic dark stage. Primary background across web, deck and video.
- **Paper — `#F4F1EC`** — a warm off-white (not pure `#FFFFFF`), for light-mode surfaces, invoices, stationery and print. Adds a premium, considered feel.
- **Graphite — `#8A8A94`** — mid grey for secondary text, captions, UI chrome, dividers.

**Optional extension (use only if range is needed)** — **Signal Dim `#C73A20`** (pressed/hover state of the accent) and **Fog `#1C1C20`** (raised surface / card on the dark canvas). Keep the chromatic family to the vermilion only; do not introduce a second hue.

**Color personality** — Confident, warm, exact. A dark room with one light on — the work is the subject, the brand is the lighting.

**Usage rules**
- Default experience is dark (Ink canvas, Paper text, Vermilion accent).
- One accent moment per screen — vermilion earns attention by being rare.
- Vermilion on Ink and Paper on Ink only; **never** vermilion text on Paper for long copy (fails contrast/legibility), and never vermilion on vermilion.
- No gradients between accent and neutral; no glows, no neon bloom; the accent is always a clean solid.
- Large vermilion fields are allowed for bold statement frames (a full-bleed accent slide / end card) — but never as a default background.

## 04 — TYPOGRAPHY

The type system *is* the "design + code are one discipline" idea made visible: a crafted grotesque for the human/design voice, a monospace for the engineering voice.

**Primary typefaces (LOCKED) — display grotesque + body grotesque.**
- **Display / headlines:** *Space Grotesk* — distinct, slightly playful geometry; the brand's headline voice.
- **Body:** *Inter* — neutral, screen-optimised, huge weight range; all paragraph and UI copy.
Character: engineered, modern, legible at any size; tight but not cramped tracking on headlines.

**Secondary typeface (LOCKED) — monospace.**
- ***JetBrains Mono*** — labels, metadata, section numbers (`01 —`), case-study specs, tags, timestamps, code and small UI detail.
Rationale: the mono is the studio's engineering fingerprint. It signals "we write the code," adds a technical texture to an otherwise clean system, and gives layouts a built-in point of difference without extra decoration.

**Type personality** — Precise, current, quietly bold. The grotesque says premium craft; the mono says we ship production code. Together they say *one team, two disciplines.*

**Usage hierarchy**
- **Headlines** — Display grotesque, large, bold, tight tracking. Short and declarative (matches the "say it straight" voice).
- **Subheads** — Display grotesque, medium weight.
- **Body** — Inter, generous line-height for readability on dark.
- **Labels / meta / specs** — Monospace, uppercase or sentence case, letter-spaced slightly, often in Graphite or Vermilion.

**Avoid** — Serifs and slab serifs (too classic/editorial for this register); humanist/rounded "friendly" fonts (undercuts precision); geometric display fonts with novelty quirks; script, handwritten or calligraphic faces; condensed "sports" grotesques; default system Arial/Helvetica as a brand face. No more than two families (grotesque + mono) plus their weights.

## 05 — IMAGERY & PHOTOGRAPHY STYLE

**Overall aesthetic** — Cinematic and dark, high-contrast, editorial. The feeling of a studio at work after hours: focused, premium, real. Imagery is *evidence of craft*, not stock mood.

**Subject matter**
- **The work, on real devices** — shipped sites and apps shown on dark screens, close crops of interface detail, motion stills.
- **Process** — design artboards beside code editors in the same frame (the dual-discipline story told literally), hands, screens, whiteboards, type specimens.
- **People, sparingly** — the founder/team candid and in-focus-on-craft, not posed corporate headshots; founders/clients only if authentic.
- **Abstract / WebGL renders** — for the hero only: restrained 3D geometry derived from the mark's grid (nodes, planes, wireframe-to-solid), used as a *stage*, never as a gradient wallpaper.

**Color treatment** — Deep shadows, controlled highlights, largely desaturated — then one deliberate vermilion element in frame (a screen glow, an object, an accent of light). Natural but graded for contrast; cinematic, not clinical.

**What to avoid** — Generic agency stock (handshakes, high-fives, diverse-team-around-laptop, sticky-note walls staged for the camera); over-produced glossy renders; AI-gradient / iridescent abstract blobs; lens flares and bokeh clichés; bright white flat-lay "startup" imagery; anything that looks like a template hero. No region-specific visual shorthand for "India" or "global."

## 06 — ICONOGRAPHY & ILLUSTRATION

**Style** — Monoline line icons on a strict grid. Sharp or minimally-rounded corners to match the mark. Geometric and constructed — the icons should look drawn by the same hand as the logo.

**Weight** — Regular (≈1.5–2px optical at base size); a single consistent stroke weight across the set. No filled/duotone mixing.

**Personality** — Primarily functional and utilitarian — icons serve clarity (the "say it straight" principle). One or two *branded* icons may carry the interaction-node detail from the logo to tie the set back to the identity. 

**Illustration** — Minimal. If used, abstract and structural: grids, nodes, vector paths, wireframe-to-solid transitions derived from the mark — never characters, mascots, or decorative spot illustration. Motion-first where possible (these are better as small animations than static art).

## 07 — DESIGN PRINCIPLES

**Craft you can feel.**
Precision is the brand. Spacing, optical alignment, grid discipline and easing curves are not polish added at the end — they are the product on display. If a detail wouldn't survive a close crop, fix it.

**One system, two disciplines.**
The grotesque-plus-mono type pairing and the converging mark exist to show that design and engineering are one team. Keep that duality legible across touchpoints; never let the identity read as "just design" or "just dev."

**Dark stage, single spotlight.**
Default to the dark canvas and spend attention deliberately — one vermilion accent, one headline, one focal image per view. Restraint is what makes it read premium; clutter makes it read template.

**Motion as proof.**
Because the studio sells web and app craft, motion must demonstrate it — purposeful, eased, exact. Everything that can earn its animation (the mark locking, the hero, interaction states) should move with intent; nothing bounces or decorates.

## 08 — BRAND EXPRESSIONS

**Website (flagship) — dark, cinematic, 3D/WebGL hero.**
Ink canvas, Paper type, one vermilion accent. Hero uses a restrained WebGL stage built from the mark's geometry (wireframe-to-solid nodes), with the logo animating in — not a gradient backdrop. Mono used for section numbers, project specs and labels. The site itself is the primary portfolio proof: performance, motion quality and detail are the pitch. Six case studies as the spine.

**Instagram & LinkedIn.**
Dark templates with a consistent grid; mono labels and section numbering give instant recognisability (the "system logic" from Nothing). LinkedIn skews to case-study breakdowns and founder POV (straight-talking, confident); Instagram skews to work, motion clips and process crops. Profile icon = the standalone symbol on Ink. One accent per post, never more.

**Pitch decks & proposals.**
Ink background default, Paper for dense/readable pages. Display grotesque headlines kept short and declarative; mono for data, scope, timelines and pricing (honest, legible, no jargon). A full-bleed vermilion statement slide reserved for the single key moment (the offer / the idea). Consistent `01 —` numbering ties decks to web and this brief.

**Business cards, email signatures, invoices, stationery.**
Print leans to the Paper off-white with Ink type and a single vermilion detail; the symbol debossed or printed solid, legible at small size. Email signature: wordmark + mono contact line, one accent link. Invoices: clean, mono for figures and line items, Paper surface — honest and premium, matching the "say it straight" value.

**Video intros/outros, favicon & app icon.**
Intro/outro: the mark draws in and locks at the node on Ink, with a restrained caret pulse and an eased motion signature reused across all video — a recognisable sonic/visual sting. Favicon & app icon: the standalone symbol, single-colour, tested at 16px and reversed (Paper-on-Ink and Ink-on-Paper) before sign-off.

## IMPLEMENTATION NOTES & OPEN DECISIONS

- **Accent colour is LOCKED: Vermilion `#FF4D2E`** — final, chosen for boldness, warmth and global neutrality. No alternative accent.
- **Typefaces are LOCKED:** *Space Grotesk* (display/headlines), *Inter* (body), *JetBrains Mono* (labels, metadata, code) — all free and license-clean for client and studio use.
- **Every mark deliverable must be proofed in four states before approval:** full-colour, single-colour, reversed (light-on-dark), and 16px. Any option that fails one of these is out.
- Nothing in this identity references or derives from any previous company; all references above (Stripe, Linear, Nothing) are inspiration-only, for specific qualities named, never to copy.
